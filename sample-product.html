The problem

You ask: "Write an email to my client about the delay."

You get back something polite, long, stiff, and useless. It doesn't sound like you. It doesn't mention the real reason for the delay. You spend ten minutes rewriting it, and you wonder why you bothered.

Why it happens

AI can't read your mind. When your request is short, it has to guess everything you left out:

  • Who is the client? A friend or a big company?
  • What happened? How late is it?
  • What do you want the client to feel or do?
  • Should it be short or detailed? Formal or friendly?

When it guesses, it picks the safest, most average answer. Average is what "generic" means.

The fix is not a magic phrase. The fix is giving it the information a smart new assistant would need on their first day.

The fix: the 5-part context formula

Before you hit Enter, check that your request has these five things. Remember them as R-G-A-F-E.

  1. Role: who should the AI act as? "You are an experienced project manager."
  2. Goal: what do you want to happen? "Keep the client calm and agree a new date."
  3. Audience: who will read it? "A long-term client who is friendly but busy."
  4. Facts: the real details only you know. "The delivery is 5 days late because our supplier shipped the wrong part. New date: March 14."
  5. Example or format: how should it look? "Under 120 words. Short paragraphs. Sound like a person, not a company."

You don't need all five every time. But every one you add makes the answer less generic.

Before and after

Before:

Write an email to my client about the delay.

After:

You are an experienced project manager. Write an email to a long-term client who is friendly but busy. Our delivery will be 5 days late because our supplier shipped the wrong part. The new date is March 14. My goal is to keep them calm and get them to confirm the new date. Keep it under 120 words, warm and direct, and end with one clear question.

The second request takes 30 seconds longer to write. It saves the 10 minutes you used to spend rewriting.

Try it now

Copy this, fill in the brackets, and send it to ChatGPT or Claude:

You are [role].
I need [what you want: an email, a plan, a post, a list].
It is for [who will read or use it].
Here are the facts: [the details only you know].
Make it [length, tone, format]. Here is an example of the style I like: [optional example].
Before you answer, ask me up to 3 questions if anything important is missing.

That last line is a secret weapon. Instead of guessing, the AI asks you what it needs to know.

Check yourself

  • ☐ I used at least 3 of the 5 parts (Role, Goal, Audience, Facts, Example).
  • ☐ I gave real details, not "the project" or "the thing".
  • ☐ I said how long it should be.
  • ☐ The answer needed little or no rewriting.

If you ticked all four, you just fixed the number one reason people give up on AI.
