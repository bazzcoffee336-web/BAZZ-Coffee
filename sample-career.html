Chapter 1: "I apply everywhere and hear nothing."

The problem

You've sent 80 applications. You've had 2 replies, both rejections. Each "apply" click feels like throwing paper into the wind.

Why it happens

Mass applying feels productive, but it usually means a general resume sent to jobs that are only partly a fit. Popular postings can get a huge number of applicants, and both software and recruiters scan quickly for a clear match. A resume that isn't clearly matched to the job is easy to skip.

The fix: fewer, better applications

  1. Set a weekly target of quality, not quantity. For example, 8 to 12 strong applications a week instead of 50 weak ones.
  2. Only apply where you match most of the must-haves. Read the requirements. If you match about two-thirds or more, apply. Nice-to-haves are optional. But skip it if you're missing a hard requirement, like a license, a work permit, or a skill you'd use every day, or one of the first items on the list (employers usually list the most important first).
  3. Tailor the top third of your resume (headline, summary, first few bullets) to each job's language. Chapter 2 shows how.
  4. Apply early. Postings are often busiest in the first days. Set job alerts so you see new roles quickly.
  5. Add a human touch. For your best-fit roles, find a person at the company and send a short message (Chapter 5).
  6. Track everything in a simple sheet: company, role, date, link, contact, status, follow-up date.
