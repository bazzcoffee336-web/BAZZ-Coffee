# 60-30-10 colour rule

Two calm colours do 90% of the work; one loud colour shows people where to look.

- **60% main**: the colour covering most of the space (background, big surfaces). It can be dark (midnight, carbon, navy, forest) or light/soft (salmon, ice blue, cream).
- **30% second**: text, cards, lines, secondary surfaces. Pairs with the main: navy+cream, black+white, forest+sand, carbon+silver, graphite+ice.
- **10% loud accent**: used rarely and only on what matters: the primary button (the thing to press), one word in the headline, one small detail (a ring, a dial line, a price). Never on surfaces or every element.

## Make the accent unexpected
The accent should not be the obvious match of the other two. Proven pairs:
- black + white -> lime green (#C8F432)
- navy + cream -> coral (#FF5A4E)
- forest green + sand beige -> electric purple (#8B5CF6)
- carbon black + pearl white -> rosso red (#D40000)
- forged carbon + titanium silver -> yellow
- midnight carbon + ivory cream -> electric azure (#1EA7FF)
- salmon + deep navy -> aqua blue (#13C8E8)
- racing green + champagne gold -> raspberry pink
- ice blue + graphite steel -> flame orange (#FF5A1F)

## How to apply
1. Name the three colours with roles and hex codes before editing.
2. Check the 10% really is ~10%: if the accent appears on more than the primary action, one headline word and one detail, remove it elsewhere.
3. Text on the accent must reach 4.5:1 contrast (use dark text on bright accents).
4. Keep a brand colour (like a logo gold) for the brand mark; the loud accent is for action.
5. Same product, different palette = different mood: show 2-3 palette options when the user is choosing.
